# Claim ledger

Keep this working artifact outside the manuscript.

| Claim ID | Source location/version | Meaning to preserve | Status and premises | Protected values/math/units/citations | Revised location or disposition | Check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| C01 | Exact paragraph, theorem or section | Scientific claim | Assumption / design / definition / exact result / approximation / empirical finding / open identification | Tokens plus interpretation | Retained / re-expressed / consolidated / relocated with destination | Source-backed / unresolved |

Track meaning, not identical sentence order.

Begin with a short argument map: broader question → narrower task → principal contribution → supporting results → applications. For each major paragraph, record its present purpose and why the reader needs it at that point. Keep this map separate from a chronology of the author's project.

For a complete document, add a section-role/dependency map, a component coverage table and a register of distinct models, regimes and interventions. Trace central claims through the abstract, results, captions, discussion and conclusion; record the source/result that supports each use. Track reused notation and whether a normalization applies per component or to the whole system. Follow the full-document workflow for integration and fresh complete-candidate review.

- Preserve value, sign, inequality, quantifier, unit, conditioning and domain.
- Preserve equation meaning and symbol binding. Prefer exact equations when moving them; explain equivalent reparameterization.
- Keep citation identities attached to supported claims. Record destinations for moved citations.
- Track historical context, prior achievements and claimed gaps separately from the author's contribution. Verify chronology and attribution; do not turn a construction chosen for this paper into an unsupported claim about what the field has never done.
- Distinguish shared premises from results belonging to one construction only.
- Record what supplied laws prescribe and what the construction demonstrates using them; do not treat an implemented input relation as an independently derived law.
- Preserve negative scope where it changes interpretation.
- Distinguish preserved information from unchanged variables.
- Record interventions as changed variables, fixed variables, law/conditioning and measured quantity.
- Audit joint-law premises separately from marginal laws; distinguish successive-use assumptions from assumptions across subsystems.
- Record each consequential change of description: full variables → operation or restriction → descriptor, with its validity premises and discarded information. Check whether a local, spatially varying or ensemble quantity has become a single state through a special preparation, averaging or factorization assumption.
- Check units, angle conventions and normalization, especially when a transformation changes the physical interpretation. Record the reason for a burdensome convention and any equivalent translation.
- Preserve the denominator and condition behind each mean, probability or normalized state. A zero-probability event has no defined conditional state.
- For an error claim, record its metric, weighting, domain, conditioning and type of guarantee (aggregate, pointwise, worst-case, normalized conditional). Record which variables are fixed first and which resources depend on the requested accuracy. Do not infer rare-case normalized accuracy from a weighted bound.
- Extract missing explanations from source with provenance. Do not invent bridges.
- For a relocated detail, name its existing source destination or provide it separately. Record why the detail is unnecessary at its former location and what interpretive scope remains locally.
- Mark repeated wording consolidated only when its substance survives.

Keep a separate issue table:

| Issue ID | Observed passage | Reader effect | Severity | Candidate repair | Disposition and evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| W01 | Exact phrase or structure | Difficulty or misreading | Critical / major / minor | Concrete edit | Resolved / inapplicable with reason / unresolved |

Use both ledgers to support judgment, then review the complete candidate afresh. Neither table substitutes for a reader who can follow the argument, identify its hierarchy and distinguish its scientific claims.
