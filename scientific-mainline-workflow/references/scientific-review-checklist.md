# Scientific Review Checklist

Use this checklist for independent review of a mutable candidate. First select
its evidence, quantifier, and stability levels using
[the evidence scope ladder](evidence-scope-ladder.md). Select only the sections
relevant to the declared claim, and do not skip a relevant NEED burden because
the artifact is primarily prose.

The checklist is not a license to turn every desirable strengthening into a
pass condition. Reviewers block on failed NEED predicates, contradictions,
vacuity, circularity, protected-boundary violations, and overclaim. Record
unpromoted SHOULD and COULD work as nonblocking deferrals.

## Identity and provenance

- Is the base commit current and correctly pinned?
- Does the review identify the exact candidate paths and hashes it covers?
- Are all frozen source files and hashes identified?
- Does the candidate preserve frozen theorem identities?
- Does it avoid modifying a frozen aggregate or source-bound file merely to
  advertise the new result?
- Are declared inputs separated from derived relations?
- Is execution-only engineering clearly separated from scientific identity?
- Is analogous or deprecated material clearly non-authoritative?

## Scope and proof burden

- Is the checkpoint name explicit?
- Are the evidence, quantifier, and stability levels declared?
- Are NEED, SHOULD, and COULD items separated?
- Does the scientific inference consume every NEED predicate and no more?
- Has the candidate proved that its successful domain is nonempty?
- Would one witness, finite family, open collar, or positive-measure prepared
  subset support the claim, or is a stronger universal quantifier genuinely
  required?
- Is finite-horizon control sufficient, or does the claim really require
  all-time/global stability?
- Are fixed backgrounds, finite bands, selected supports, phenomenological
  coefficients, or other effective assumptions stated honestly?
- Does any effective assumption make the mechanism circular, or is its deeper
  derivation legitimately deferred?
- Has a SHOULD or COULD item been promoted without user, steering, or proved
  dependency authority?
- Does the stop rule prevent the artifact from drifting into a stronger theorem
  after the NEED gate is met?
- Is the proof burden proportional to the active program stage?

## Types and ontology

- Is every variable's mathematical and physical type explicit at the level used
  by the claim?
- Are ontic state, derived coordinates, shadows, records, and statistical laws
  kept distinct?
- Are added fields or modes already licensed, or does the candidate silently
  add ontology?
- Are quantum, classical, PDE, reduced, and effective descriptions connected by
  an explicit map, approximation statement, or theorem where the claim uses the
  connection?
- Is exactly one actuality or record-selection mechanism used?
- Are excluded sectors named as fixed, inactive, screened, or deferred rather
  than silently removed?

## Mathematics

- Are the domains, charts, regularity, boundary conditions, and seam maps needed
  by the selected claim explicit?
- Are signs, orientations, symplectic forms, adjoints, and normalizations
  correct?
- Are compactness, boundedness, openness, positive measure, and
  finite-dimensionality used correctly?
- Does every inverse or projection act on the complete space relevant to the
  NEED claim, with excluded directions named and justified?
- Are exact identities recomputed independently where they are load-bearing?
- Are expected values derived independently from frozen source data rather than
  copied from the constructor's allowlist or table?
- Are approximate or numerical claims supplied with prospectively defined error
  budgets and the correct evidence label?
- Are denominators, lower eigenvalue bounds, decision margins, and successful
  domains proved positive when the claim needs them?
- For repeated or generated cases, is there a genuine template or substitution
  theorem whose hypotheses cover every generated NEED row?
- Does the candidate avoid presenting a numerical witness as a global analytic
  theorem?
- Does it avoid demanding exact arithmetic or exhaustive enumeration when a
  shorter controlled argument proves the same NEED predicate?

## Existence and stability

- What stability level is actually claimed: S0, S1, S2, S3, or S4?
- Does the candidate establish existence for the declared solution, branch,
  trajectory, or prepared domain?
- Does it survive the observation horizon required by the claim?
- If robustness is claimed, is the collar or basin nonempty and quantitatively
  controlled?
- If local energetic, spectral, or orbital stability is claimed, is the correct
  reduced space and symmetry quotient used?
- Is global/all-data stability being demanded only when the maintained claim is
  genuinely E4/Q3/S4?
- Are inactive directions and unresolved release modes stated as assumptions or
  deferrals rather than treated as already stable?

## Dynamics and physical completeness

- Is the law local at its declared level, or is any nonlocality stated rather
  than hidden?
- Is gauge covariance or invariance proved where it is load-bearing?
- Are the conserved quantities and reciprocal reactions needed by the active
  mechanism retained?
- Are load-bearing maps, inverses, seam reactions, and source substitutions
  explicit mathematical data rather than prose assertions?
- Are losing, radiative, exhaust, seam, archive, or complement sectors retained
  when the selected claim depends on reversibility, conservation, or local
  no-signalling?
- Does a proposed detector actually capture, commit, persist, and expose the
  readable record claimed by the checkpoint?
- Are record domains robust and nonvacuous at the selected evidence level?
- Is a live execution left untouched unless the candidate explicitly governs
  its disposition?
- Is broader physical completeness correctly placed in SHOULD or COULD when the
  checkpoint only needs a selected effective model?

## Conditioning and statistics

- Is the complete unconditional alphabet for the declared operational claim
  reported first?
- Are misses, ties, nulls, dark events, faults, and unresolved histories that
  occur on the declared domain retained?
- Is any successful-domain conditional law secondary and explicitly labeled?
- Is the preparation measure declared, derived, selected, or restricted in a
  way consistent with the evidence level?
- Are source weights absent from detector gains, thresholds, and fault handling?
- Are asymptotic convergence, finite-run error, and one-shot preparation kept
  distinct?
- Does a selected finite instrument family suffice, or has arbitrary-instrument
  closure been promoted without need?

## Bell and no-signalling

- Is the operational algebra prospectively fixed for the Bell claim actually
  being made?
- Are admitted observables gauge-invariant and physically implementable?
- Does the no-signalling statement cover the complete declared local record,
  timing, and fault histories?
- Is any excluded observable physically screened, gauge variant, outside the
  declared local support, globally nonlocal, or otherwise excluded for a
  theorem-backed reason?
- Are measurement independence, future-setting independence, and
  setting-independent sampling protected?
- Is the Bell statistic unconditional or governed by a loophole-safe inequality?
- Does the violation remain positive after all declared error and null-event
  charges?
- Is the exact Bell-assumption failure stated without vague “nonlocality”
  language?
- Is the setting family appropriately scoped? A selected quartet may support an
  E2 Bell checkpoint; all settings are a stronger E3/E4 claim.
- Has the reviewer avoided demanding relativistic microcausality from a declared
  nonrelativistic preferred-time checkpoint?

## Claim discipline

- Is this an exact theorem, conditional theorem, witness, controlled numerical
  result, obstruction, regression, or selected-realization result?
- Does the inference stay within the proof's domain and horizon?
- Is hosting distinguished from emergence?
- Is finite operational closure distinguished from relativistic or spacelike
  completion?
- Is a selected realization failure kept distinct from a family no-go?
- Are stronger physical provenance, empirical calibration, and axiom
  minimization left as separate questions?
- Does the artifact close the active load-bearing question without silently
  promoting nonblocking polish into a pass condition?
- Are effective assumptions and explicit deferrals visible in the disposition?
- Would a reader understand exactly what can be used downstream and what cannot?

## Controls and validation

- Does every NEED decision predicate have at least one genuine semantic
  corruption test or an independent recomputation capable of detecting failure?
- Are SHOULD and COULD items tested only when they are actually implemented or
  promoted?
- Can the relevant controls detect omitted or duplicate objects, sign reversals,
  nonpositive bounds, incomplete active reactions, invalid support transport,
  false dependency expansion, and target-table injection?
- Are representation-only changes accepted?
- Are matched negative controls scientifically relevant to the selected claim?
- Can the validator catch missing coordinates, wrong signs, omitted declared
  faults, changed supports, postselection, and hidden narrowing?
- Are exact scalar types serialized deterministically where exactness is part of
  the theorem?
- Does the validator avoid circular agreement with the builder by recomputing
  decisive expectations through an independent route?
- Are local checks supplemented by inherited validators only when their
  hypotheses are actually consumed?
- Are validator and mutation counts described as process evidence rather than
  theorem strength?
- Is the validation suite no larger than necessary to protect the NEED claim?

## Review disposition

Return exactly one:

- `ACCEPT` — every NEED predicate is positively satisfied at the declared
  evidence, quantifier, and stability levels; list SHOULD and COULD items as
  nonblocking deferrals;
- `BLOCK` — list the smallest concrete corrections to a NEED predicate,
  contradiction, vacuity, circularity, protected boundary, or overclaim;
- `FAIL SELECTED REALIZATION` — the reviewed candidate cannot meet its declared
  NEED gate without a new identity;
- `CLASS-LEVEL OBSTRUCTION` — only with a theorem covering the declared class.

Do not return `BLOCK` merely because a stronger, broader, more elegant, or more
fundamental result would be desirable.

State surviving positive lemmas even when the overall candidate fails. Bind the
disposition to the reviewed base commit, candidate artifact bytes and hashes,
manifest, evidence level, quantifier level, stability level, NEED set and
predicates, assumptions, validation, prepared domain, and inference. Any change
to one of those binding identities invalidates the disposition. Inspect the
revised artifact and issue a new disposition rather than carrying forward the
old acceptance.
